OpenRoad

Privacy policy

Last updated 30 October 2026

OpenRoad is free motorbike navigation. It has no accounts, no adverts and no analytics, and the developer runs no servers that collect your data. What you plan, record and type stays on your phone, except for the requests to the map, routing and weather services listed below, which OpenRoad needs to work.

What stays on your phone

Your routes, trips, recorded rides, ride diary and photos, bike and maintenance details, settings, emergency (ICE) card, and check-in and crash-alert contact are stored only on your phone. They are not sent to the developer. Uninstalling OpenRoad deletes them, unless you have saved or shared copies yourself (for example a GPX file or a PDF).

Location

OpenRoad uses your phone's precise location to show where you are, give turn-by-turn directions, record your rides and warn you about what's ahead. While you navigate or record a ride it keeps doing this with the screen off, shown by a notification. It does not track you when you're not using these features, and your location history is not sent to the developer.

To plan routes and show the map, OpenRoad sends the services below the places involved (for example your start, stops and destination, or the area on screen). These services receive your phone's internet address, as with any website.

Group rides and shared hazards

In a group ride, your position, name as you set it, messages and shared routes are sent to the other riders through free public MQTT servers (HiveMQ and EMQX). Everything is encrypted with a key made from the group code, so the servers only see unreadable data; anyone with the group code can read it, so share the code only with your group. Bike-to-bike sharing over Bluetooth works the same way, between phones nearby.

If you turn on hazards from all OpenRoad riders, hazards you mark (such as gravel or diesel) are shared with every OpenRoad rider nearby, with their position and a random code for your phone, but no name. They are deleted after 6 hours.

Safety check-in and crash detection

If you set a contact and miss a check-in, or the crash alarm isn't cancelled, OpenRoad prepares a message to that contact with your position (and, for a crash, the medical notes from your emergency card if you added them). The Google Play version opens your messaging app with the text ready to send; the version from GitHub sends the text itself. In a group ride, your group is also told.

Permissions

  • Location: navigation, ride recording, warnings ahead.
  • Microphone: voice commands, only when you start one.
  • Nearby devices (Bluetooth): bike-to-bike group positions with no signal, only if you turn it on.
  • Notifications, alarms: turn alerts, check-in and crash alarms, ride recording.
  • Camera (through the QR scanner): scanning a group ride's code.

Children

OpenRoad is made for adult motorcyclists and isn't directed at children.
